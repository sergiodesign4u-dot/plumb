Two people, and what we actually know about them

Personas here are behavioural hypotheses with a named source, not portraits. There is no primary data about our users yet, so every line traces either to the brief, which is our own assumption, or to an artefact somebody else made. Both are marked.

P1

The reader who is about to be quoted

Primary

Context

PM, marketer or finance, 25 to 45, non-technical. Arrives from exposure, not from curiosity: a deck due, a meeting within the hour, a decision with their name on it. hypothesis, from the brief. How often this happens and what surface they read in are both unknown.

What it answers: the flow does not start at a home page or a search box, it starts at one number opened from a link. That answer is an inference drawn from a brief-only trigger, and it decides the first screen of the primary flow.

Updated after publication, CJM step 4

The inference above assumes the link is opened. The first outside evidence bearing on that assumption points the other way. In a comparative trial inside a healthcare organisation, push beat pull, and a dashboard that was linked directly within the EHR, didn't require a login, and was contextualized for the provider (basically as low friction as you could get was visited 1-2 times per year.

Hacker News comment 28330744, dated 2021-08-27, screen research/screens/cjm-hn-28330744-push-vs-pull.png. Corrected at CJM step 6: what matches our design is the delivery conditions, not the object, since theirs is a dashboard to explore and ours is one number with its provenance. It reports rare entry, one to two times a year, not zero. Population is clinicians rather than B2B business readers.

Jobs

Canonical wording, identical to jtbd.md. No job exists in two editions.

  • Main. When I have to put a number in front of other people, I want to know how far it can be trusted, so that I am not defending something I cannot back up.
  • R1. When a number does not match what I expected, I want to find out who is answerable for it, so that I can ask a specific person rather than guess who to ask.
  • R4. When I come back to a number I saved earlier, I want to know whether anything about it changed since, so that an old figure does not travel as a current one. hypothesis, nothing shows that anyone reopens a saved link.

Pains

The number arrives stripped of everything that would let them judge it. Provenance is a destination that has to be chosen, and choosing it requires already suspecting something. The state vocabulary exists in the market and sits where they have no reason to go. Where access is given, the free role sees least.

Market, cited in full in personas.md.

Updated after publication, CJM step 4

That provenance is what a non-technical reader is actually asking for now has a source outside vendor marketing. A practitioner reporting on interviews with clinicians: when clinicians ask for more data, they're actually asking for more lineage or provenance metadata.

Hacker News comment 28331531, dated 2021-08-27, screen research/screens/cjm-hn-28331531-provenance-attribution.png. Adjacent population, so it supports the shape of the pain rather than proving it for our reader.

Choice triggers

Working assumption, not an observation: they do not choose the product, they choose whether to trust this number. A reader can also simply refuse the tool, and nothing rules that out.

What we expect to convince them, derived from how other products score in the benchmark rather than from anything a reader said: a name and a date on the claim, an age in relative time, a named state instead of a red dash. What we expect to repel: a bare figure, a trust score that cannot be interrogated, a login wall.

hypothesis The whole list decides what the card carries, which makes it the most expensive unverified line in this file.

Demographic facts, each with its consequence

  • Non-technical, so depth on request must be legible without knowledge of the stack. The footed half is that a lineage graph requires a mental model of the stack; that this specific reader fails that bar is unverified.
  • Unpaid, so they cannot be asked to create an account. That is where the account-free card decision comes from.

still missing No verbatim reader voice exists in the research and none will be synthesised. The follow-up research found analysts talking about business readers, never a business reader talking. Stage 05 needs a voice, and the only route left to it is talking to one.

P2

The data lead who is asked

Secondary

Context

28 to 40, data team of 1 to 5 inside a company of 50 to 300. Arrives on somebody else's trigger: an inbound "why is this number different", several times a week. hypothesis, from the brief.

What it answers: their flow starts from an interruption, so the entry screen is search and jump to a metric, not a dashboard. Same standing as P1's entry, an inference from a brief-only trigger.

Jobs

  • R2. When the same question about a number reaches me again, I want to answer it once and have the answer keep answering, so that I stop being the only route to it.
  • R3. When I change what a number means, I want the people who already used it to find out, so that my correction reaches the decisions it affects.
  • R5. When I bring a new metric under control, I want to state once what it means and who owns it, so that the definition stops living in somebody's head.

R2 is the analyst's job of main-job weight. jtbd.md records the test for why it is not a second main job.

Pains

Manual digging per question. Being a human API to their own data. A reputation that depends on their availability. From the market: definition governance lives in producer tools, certification is a badge, and a name appears only when the badge is removed.

Choice triggers

Whether the cost can be judged before a conversation: seven of the ten HARD and SOFT products publish no seat price. Whether the product demands access to rows, where metadata-only is our argument.

Market. Corrected during the critique: no public price proves cost cannot be compared without sales, not that the product cannot be evaluated at all.

Demographic facts, each with its consequence

  • Team of 1 to 5, so we expect no rota and every question landing on the same person, which means the product has to answer in their absence. The team size is from the brief; the absence of a rota is inference.
  • Company of 50 to 300. Whether a data steward role exists at that size is unknown, and the earlier claim that it does not was invented and has been removed. Ownership as one name rather than a workflow is therefore a design decision, not a finding.

Mood quote

dbt Community Forum, thread dated 2019

"If there is a question you are getting asked frequently and you find yourself pulling the same data for multiple users, then it almost always is a good use of your time to set that up as a dashboard"

A real practitioner voice rather than a synthesis, and it carries a warning as well as a tone: the default answer to R2 in the field is to build a dashboard, not to write a definition. That is our real competitor for that job.

□

Not a persona: the buyer

Head of data or COO. Deliberately given no product surface. They still impose constraints: a trust surface in the IA, SOC 2 Type II, SSO, and the metadata-only claim, plus the open question of whether an account-free card survives a security review.

Recorded as a constraint rather than a persona, and that is not a formality. A third persona would collect its own jobs, its own screens at the IA stage and its own share of MVP scope, and five stages later nobody would remember it was never meant to have any.

What the primary mark does

A conflict of decisions is resolved in favour of the reader: they carry the higher risk and have fewer levers, since they cannot change the definition, cannot see the pipeline and often cannot log in. The analyst's registry has to be good; it does not get to decide the shape of the card. The price of the choice, stated plainly: the interface is built around the person who never pays.
