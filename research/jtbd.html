Plumb · Stage 02 · User Research

What these people are actually trying to get done

One main job, five related, and the matrix that turns them into an MVP core of two. A job here names a person's progress, never a mechanism: the test applied to each one is whether a completely different product could be hired for it.

Assembled 2026-08-11 Wording is canonical and mirrored verbatim in the personas Source: research/docs/jtbd.md
01

The main job

MainP1, the readergap evidenced by the market
When I have to put a number in front of other people, I want to know how far it can be trusted, so that I am not defending something I cannot back up.

Support, stated precisely. The market evidences the gap, not the hiring. That a reader will actually do this is the riskiest assumption of the whole product, and the first test exists to settle it.

Solution-agnostic check. No mechanism is named. A certification badge, a status board, a conversation with the analyst or a mail digest could each be hired for it. Our card is an answer to the job, not part of its statement.

Why there is one main job and not two

The analyst has a job of the same weight, R2, answer once and have the answer keep answering. Two main jobs usually mean two products, so the test was run rather than assumed away.

The test: does serving one discharge the other?

It does. If a reader can judge a number without asking anyone, the analyst stops being the only route to the answer by construction. These are the same event seen from two sides, not two events. This is a decision rather than a fact, and if it is wrong the consequence lands early: the CJM takes the focus of one persona times one main job, and the IA builds navigation for it.

02

Related jobs

Adjacent work in the same context, not steps toward the main job. Steps of a journey already have an owner in this pipeline: the phases of the CJM, which runs next.

R1P1, the readermarket
When a number does not match what I expected, I want to find out who is answerable for it, so that I can ask a specific person rather than guess who to ask.

Attribution scores 3 or below in all five benchmark products while several score 5 on freshness. PowerMetrics names a person only when certification is removed. The market's own model of doubt has a human owner: a Sifflet incident carries an assignee.

R2P2, the data leadmarket plus a practitioner voice
When the same question about a number reaches me again, I want to answer it once and have the answer keep answering, so that I stop being the only route to it.

Definition governance lives in a producer tool and the last mile is delegated to a surface owned by somebody else, which is the structural reason the analyst keeps being asked. The follow-up research lifted this job off our brief: a practitioner names the repeated question in his own words, and the same sentence says the field's default answer is to build a dashboard. A dashboard, not a competitor product, is what this job is hired against today.

R3P2, the data leadbrief for the behaviour
When I change what a number means, I want the people who already used it to find out, so that my correction reaches the decisions it affects.

Half of this job is unserved by design: the card will say the definition changed when somebody opens it, and there is nothing to push, because notification routing is deliberately out of MVP. It is the same root that made retention the weakest stage of the funnel.

R4P1, the readerhypothesis
When I come back to a number I saved earlier, I want to know whether anything about it changed since, so that an old figure does not travel as a current one.

There is no evidence that anyone ever reopens a saved link. Kept deliberately, despite being the closest of the five to the main job: the whole retention mechanic stands on it, and dropping it would leave that stage with no work underneath it at all.

R5P2, the data leadmarket for the shape
When I bring a new metric under control, I want to state once what it means and who owns it, so that the definition stops living in somebody's head.

The metric as a first-class object is a real product shape and the whole category sells it. That vendors build it is evidence; that an analyst wants the definition out of their head is our inference. Activation depends on this job having been done and is not this job: activation is measured when a second person opens a card.

The two pairs. Main and R2 are the same event from two sides, and so are R3 and R4: the analyst propagates a change, the reader meets it on re-reading. Both pairs are kept because the personas differ and the progress differs. Naming them here stops a later stage from treating one as a duplicate and deleting it.

MVP core: two jobs, not three

  1. The main job. Importance 3 for the primary persona, not closed by the market. The cell that justifies it: provenance is a destination, so telling whether a number can be trusted where it is read is served by nobody in this set.
  2. R1, who is answerable. Importance 3 for the primary persona, not closed. The market proved the habit of showing age and did not prove the habit of showing who is accountable.

A third, conditional: R4. It qualifies on both columns, but its own importance is unverified because the job itself is a hypothesis. If the first cohort shows that saved numbers are not revisited, R4 leaves the core and the retention mechanic leaves with it.

Padding to three was refused. R2 is discharged by the main job succeeding. R3 scores 2 for the primary and is the sending side of R4. R5 is outside the core for the opposite reason: the market already closes it. The product needs a registry, but the core is what we have to win on, not what we have to have.

Orphan features. Not one in the mandated inventory, which says the scope set at stage 01 was assembled around work people do. Two exist just outside it, both from the funnel: a reader inviting another reader, and the public page rendering the real card. They serve business outcomes rather than anybody's job, and naming that is what will hold the priority when the IA starts handing out screens.

Refined at the CJM stage, and this is the current version of the core

The two jobs did not change. What changed is the capability list underneath them. One item was cut as an orphan, the full lineage tree, which sat under a "later" label with "analyst depth" as its parent, and that is neither a barrier nor a job. Two capabilities were missing rather than vague and were added: a definition snapshot taken at read time, without which the state "definition changed after this was saved" has nothing to compare against, and sending a metric as a link in one action, without which the card never reaches anybody. The provenance split was corrected against the project's own principle that the definition is the main object: the definition is visible on the card, and what opens on request is the source.

A build-order fact for the IA stage, not a scope change. The first test of the riskiest assumption needs four of the eleven capabilities: the card at a permanent URL, the named states, the visible definition with the source on request, and sending it as a link. Hand-made static pages have no live source and no second definition. Four capabilities carry the entire risk.

05

Critique and risks

Both files were criticised in two instruments. Codex ran first, blind and read-only, checking them against the foundation research; a parallel pass covered the classes that need the product rather than the text. Sixteen findings, all repaired. Codex found 9 that the first pass missed, the first pass found 3 he could not see, 4 were found by both.

What the second instrument bought

All five divergences were the same mistake in different places: market evidence for a gap was written up as market evidence for a person's behaviour. One class of correction ran deeper: importance scores in the matrix were justified by our own MVP scope, which is evidence about us and not about how much a job matters to anyone. Nothing was withdrawn at verification this time, but one finding was accepted with a correction to its reasoning rather than whole: the research does carry the footing Codex said was missing, just not for our specific reader.
