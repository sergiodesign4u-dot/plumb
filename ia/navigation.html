Global navigation, and the column that is empty on purpose

The first structural node, and the one that sets the level of detail for the other thirty-six. Two destinations, no hamburger, no bottom tab bar, and nothing at all for the person this product is built around.

Type: section Scope: MVP Source: ia/docs/pages/navigation.md
01

The decision this node records

The guest state of this component is empty, and that is the design

The reader has no global navigation. It follows from the strongest measurement in this research: a destination a reader is expected to travel to gets entered once or twice a year, so what we build has to arrive with the number rather than wait to be visited. A header with a logo and a menu on the number card would turn the card into a page of a site the reader has no reason to be on.

So this component serves the secondary persona almost entirely. The analyst signs in and gets two destinations. A guest sees the card, the footer, and nothing else, including no invitation to sign in: offering an account to somebody who will never have one is noise, and the route to signing in lives on the public product page and in the footer.

02

Why this shape, argued rather than assumed

Three sources, opened 2026-08-11
FindingSourceWhat it decides here
"If your site has more than 5 options, it's hard to fit them in a tab or navigation bar and still keep an optimum touch-target size", and tab bars are "well suited for sites with relatively few navigation options"Nielsen Norman Group, mobile navigation patternsTwo destinations, far below the ceiling. Fit is not our problem
"The navigation menu makes the navigation options least discoverable", and out of sight is out of mindNielsen Norman Group, same articleNo hamburger. With two destinations, hiding them costs discoverability and buys nothing
Target size minimum is "at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels", Level AA; the enhanced criterion is "at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels", Level AAAW3C, Understanding WCAG 2.2, SC 2.5.8 and SC 2.5.5Targets here are built to 44px, the AAA figure, because the cost of doing so is zero
A deliberate departure from the usual answer

The obvious mobile pattern is a bottom tab bar of four or five items. We are not building one. With two destinations plus an account corner, a bottom bar would spend the most valuable strip of a phone screen on almost nothing, and the analyst's dense work belongs on the desktop while the phone is the read and verify path. The phone case that actually matters here is the reader opening a card, and the reader has no navigation at all. One top bar at both widths, and the bottom of the phone screen stays free for content.

05

SEO and accessibility

  • No H1. A global component never carries the page heading.
  • Indexing: every screen carrying this component is behind authentication and is noindex. The component therefore never appears on an indexed page and declares no schema.
  • Internal linking: this is the main plane of it for the signed-in product, and it is a small plane, two links. The public surface links through the footer instead, which is why the footer is node 0.2 rather than an afterthought.
  • Semantics: one nav with an accessible name, aria-current="page" on the active destination, and the account menu as a button with aria-expanded rather than a link.
  • Targets: 44 by 44 CSS pixels.
  • No breadcrumbs. The analyst's tree is two levels deep, and breadcrumbs on two levels are furniture.

Open questions this node hands forward

  1. Whether the wordmark should link anywhere at all. It currently goes to the registry because that is where a signed-in analyst starts, and a product with no home page has no obvious answer.
  2. What the account menu does when a workspace has one analyst, which is the common shape at 50 to 300 people. Answered at step 8, because the audit made it a defect rather than a question: an MVP component may not offer routes to nodes that do not exist in MVP, so in MVP the corner carries sign out and nothing else.
