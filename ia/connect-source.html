The one form whose fix is on the other side of a wall we cannot see

Credentials, network policy and grants live with the customer's data team or their IT. A form that reports "connection failed" and stops has handed somebody a dead end whose route out is not on our screen, which is why this node spends most of its length on the failure.

02

The cadence field, and the hole underneath it

We hold no cache, so there is nothing to expire. PowerMetrics gets freshness from a webhook the customer calls or from a cache TTL they pick. What we need from the same place is the expected cadence, so that "as of 14:05" has something to be measured against. Node 0.4 handed this question forward and the domain answered it: cadence is configured, not discovered.

So the expected cadence is a claim, not a measurement, and it is the only value typed on this page that the reader ever sees. It appears in the state line on the card and as a row in node 2.7, which makes it the field on this form with the largest consequence per keystroke.

The open question this creates, named rather than hidden

Nothing checks the declared cadence against what the source actually does. Verifying it needs a history of run times, and we keep one overwritten timestamp rather than a history, which is the same architectural choice that keeps deep version history out of scope. So a connection can declare hourly and answer daily, and the card will say "expected hourly" in good faith.

That is a real hole in the main job's promise. It is carried as an open question rather than repaired by inventing storage this stage has no authority to add.

The constraint from the bank that turns out not to apply

The bank took a constraint from PowerMetrics: first day of week and time zone must match the warehouse, or weekly figures are wrong. It was taken because a silent wrong number is exactly the failure this product sells against.

Read against our own architecture, most of it does not apply. PowerMetrics needs those settings because it builds queries. We do not: the analyst pastes a query that already exists and we show the one value it returns. We never aggregate, so we have no week to start and no calendar to get wrong.

What survives lands somewhere else. A definition can still be wrong about its own week, and that is a sentence in the analyst's own words at node 4.4, not a setting on a connection. So this form gains no field, and the bank row is re-read rather than mechanically obeyed: a settings pair added here would be two more ways to produce a wrong number in a product whose whole claim is the opposite.

03

The map said two halves, and testing finds three

The node map describes 3.4 as "which half failed, credentials or reachability". Testing a warehouse produces three distinct failures, and they matter because the fix is in a different place each time.

What failedWhat the form saysWhere the fix is

We reached it and were refusedThat the host answered and rejected the identityThe credential fields, and the secret is the first thing named

This is a refinement inside the node rather than a change to the map, and it earns its place because a single "connection failed" sends a person to the wrong wall two times out of three.

The secret field, and the conflict it creates with a rule we already set

The form stays filled in after a failure, which is a rule taken from the bank and holds everywhere in this product. But a secret cannot be refilled, because we never send it back: a private key or a password is written to us and never read out, not even masked, which is the same argument the sources list makes about showing credentials.

Resolution, stated rather than left to an implementer. The non-secret fields keep their values. The secret field shows that a value is set rather than showing characters, and a failure the analyst did not cause does not clear it. On an authentication failure it is named as the first thing to check, and replacing it is one action.

04

Editing, saving, and where this page sends people

Changing credentials means every metric on this connection stops answering until the test passes again. R8's banner shape carries it: their "changing these details will require a reconnect" becomes ours, "8 metrics read from this connection and will stop answering until this connects again". The number is nameable here, exactly as at 3.1 and unlike 4.7, because metrics are our objects and readings are not.

Delete follows the rule set at 3.1: a connection carrying metrics cannot be removed, and the confirmation says how many and what to do about them.

States, and where each one goes
NodeStateWhat happens
3.2New, or a connection that existsThe same form. Editing adds the consequence banner
3.3TestingProgress in place, naming which half is being tried. Not a modal and not a success window: the success of a connection is worth saying once, in place
3.4FailedBanner at the top, inline message on the field that caused it, and the form still filled in
SavedThe first connection in a workspace goes to 4.2, the empty registry. A returning one goes to 3.1

Save is enabled only after a successful test. Test then save is the order, because save-then-discover is the version that produces a broken source sitting in a list. The whole form as a modal is refused: nine fields in a modal at 360 is a scroll inside a scroll, and the map already says this is a page.

Nothing from the emotional and social table lands on this page

Written rather than padded, for the same reason as node 3.1: this is infrastructure. One adjacent note, and it is not a mechanism. The prerequisites panel exists so the commonest failure here, a network policy the analyst does not control, is visible before the attempt rather than after it. That removes one handover to another team, but nothing in the research measures it, so it stays a design intention.

Responsive. At 360: the warehouse choice, the prerequisites, the fields, the actions. The panel goes above the fields it explains, because information that unblocks a field is useless after it, and the action row is sticky since a nine-field form is longer than a phone. At width: a single capped column with the panel beside it, R7's three-part layout reduced to two because we have no environment list.

SEO: noindex, no schema, no SEO text. Private and transactional, and it names the customer's infrastructure.
