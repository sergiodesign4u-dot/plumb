An open question closed here. Node 0.4 asked what "expected hourly" says when no cadence is known. The domain answer is that freshness is configured, not discovered: PowerMetrics learns it from a webhook the customer calls or a cache TTL the customer picks. So the expected-cadence field the base layer put in the connection form is a requirement rather than an assumption. The other half, how old is old, stays open.

One silent-wrong-number generator worth naming. That is precisely the failure this product sells against, so in ours it is a stated assumption of the connection rather than a setting somebody has to go and find.

T4, the gate: the first type whose domain half is public

Competitor sign-in pages need no account to look at, so both domain sources here were opened live rather than read about, and the comparison column is a real comparison instead of a barrier.

SourceKindWhat is actually on the page
dbt Cloud sign-inDomain, live, HARD competitorHeading, a notice for people arriving from the extension, email, password, reset link, Continue, a legal line, a route to create a company account. No SSO control anywhere on the page
Cube Cloud sign-inDomain, live, SOFT competitorLanguage selector, email, password, Forgot password, Sign in, GitHub, Google, and last a "single sign-on" link that reveals a field. SSO is fifth

The comparison, stated plainly. Both competitors sell into US B2B, where the research records that SSO is asked about on the first call. In one the SSO route is fifth and in the other it is not on the page. Ours is the only route, and there is no password field to fail on. That is not austerity: no password means no reset, no expiry and no rotation, which is three fewer nodes than a conventional gate.

One line worth copying from dbt verbatim: "By signing in, you agree to dbt Cloud's Terms of Service unless a prior agreement is in effect." At this company size the contract is usually signed before anybody logs in, and boilerplate that ignores that is wrong on its face.

T8, the system pages: where a competitor's 404 is the argument

The reader's only entrance is a link, so a broken or expired link is their first contact with us, not a rare edge. That is why this type earns real sources rather than a shrug.

SourceKindWhat is on the page
dbt docs 404Domain, live, negative referenceIn full: "Page Not Found", "We could not find what you were looking for.", and "Please contact the owner of the site that linked you to the original URL and let them know their link is broken." No onward route of any kind. The page hands the problem to the one person who cannot solve it
Cube 404Domain, liveThe full marketing header, "Go to Homepage", and a footer carrying the whole sitemap. Twenty-five links is not a route, it is a shrug. Also: the message is an H2 and the page has no H1
Secoda in-app not-foundDomain, live, found while collecting T4"The resource can't be found. Secoda may be out of sync with the source or the resource may not exist in this workspace." It names two causes and does not pretend to know which, which is the same discipline the state vocabulary runs on
Zapier 404Craft, outside the categoryMessage, one line, one named action. Three blocks is the whole page. Their label "Back to Safety" we refuse: it does not say where it goes

One node, two renderings, and that is a finding rather than a preference. Signed out, the reader gets the message alone, with the two honest causes named and the one thing they can actually do. Signed in, the analyst gets the same message inside the shell with navigation intact, because somebody who mistypes a URL should not lose their way around.

The 500 gets a block that appears in none of the sources: what is safe to assume about the number they were looking at. The answer is nothing, because we did not get it. An error page in a trust product has to say whether what it showed can still be believed.

Real data for the cookie question, which was left as a named discrepancy. dbt Labs runs a full consent platform on its marketing site, with "Accept All Cookies", "Personalize My Choices" and a searchable vendor list. Secoda shows a plain "We use cookies" banner. Cube shows a US visitor nothing at all. Three live US B2B data companies, and two of the three show a banner, so the ПОТІМ label on the cookie notice is a genuine choice rather than an oversight, and step 7 gets evidence instead of an assumption. What stays refused is the shape: a modal about tracking on top of a card that promises we store nothing about the reader would be the worst possible first contact.

Both competitors ship effectively no product structured data. That is a cheap and real advantage in the layer this stage owns, and it is exactly the kind of thing that is invisible from a screenshot and only appears because the page was opened.

The row this type turns on. Every source puts a product screenshot or a framed demo image beside the headline. Ours is the real card component, live, with a real metric in it. A product whose claim is that a number carries its provenance can prove that claim in its own hero instead of illustrating it.

Two refusals worth naming. dbt opens on corporate news and Secoda opens on borrowed authority ("As seen on TV"). Borrowed authority is the opposite of what this product sells, since we are asking people to stop trusting numbers because of where they appeared.

What this bank does not cover

Both deferred types were collected in the ПОТІМ round and are in the bank: T5, account settings, whose domain half stays a named barrier because no competitor exposes a settings screen without an account; and T7, content, legal and support, which got its own domain pass and a template map rather than a composition. T6 was also extended with a live pass over both competitors' pricing pages and both trust centres.

The confidence in this bank is not even, and that is carried forward rather than smoothed over. For T1, T2 and T3, which hold 21 of the MVP nodes, the domain half was documentation rather than product, because the surface is behind a login we do not pass. Documentation describes an interface and shows selected screenshots of it. Density, behaviour at a narrow viewport, and what a screen feels like in the hand remain unknown for those three, so stage 04 should treat their compositions as structure rather than as evidence about experience. For T4, T8 and T6 the competitor page was opened live, and only there does the comparison column mean what it says.

Was → became

Step 8, in two instruments over both axes, then a second pass over every node the repairs touched. Codex ran first and blind, read-only, without seeing our own table; our own pass ran in parallel and was mechanical where it could be, counting node rows, template sections, cross-references and scope dependencies. Thirteen findings in the first pass and nine in the second, every one verified in the file before anything was repaired.
