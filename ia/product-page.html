The one page we are allowed to index, and its hero runs the product

The reference node of this stage. It is not a Home, because this product deliberately has none, and it is the only indexed page in a map of forty-one nodes. Both facts make it the only place the SEO layer can attach.

01

Why the reference node is 6.1 and not a Home

The template for this stage normally starts from the Home page. This product has no Home, and that is a decision the IA already took and defended: the reader arrives by a link, holds no account, and has no header, no menu and no home by design. Global navigation belongs to the analyst. Inventing a node 0.0 to satisfy a template would contradict a decision made two steps ago.

The second reason is arithmetic. Of forty-one nodes, the number card is public but noindex, because indexing it would publish customers' numbers, and everything else is private or transactional. So the structural SEO layer, which belongs to this stage rather than to production, has exactly one subject.

What this page serves, said without dressing it up

No job. Cluster 6 is jobless by construction and the traceability matrix leaves it out of the job table on purpose. This page serves acquisition, which is a business outcome rather than somebody's work. It also carries one growth mechanic: the public page renders the real card component.

The alternatives are a placeholder or a hidden block, and both would mean that the one page claiming we never hide a broken number does exactly that. A marketing page demonstrating honesty about a number it could not fetch is the product working, not the page failing.

Emotional support: none. The four mechanisms in the emotional and social table sit on the card, the send action, the definition editor and the registry. None lands here, and this line records that the check was made rather than leaving a gap.

04

SEO block A to E

A. Meta tags, with finished copy

title (48): Plumb: every number with its source and owner
description (147): Plumb keeps the definition of every metric in one place and shows, right beside the number, where it came from, how fresh it is and who declared it.
canonical: absolute form of / on the production domain
hreflang: none, deliberately. One language, English
robots: index, follow
og: title, description, type=website, url=canonical, image = a rendering of the card
twitter: summary_large_image

The canonical is specified by rule, not by string, because the production domain is not decided: runtime hosting is explicitly still open. A hreflang cluster of one is noise, so there is none. And the og:image is a decision rather than a default: a logo card would repeat exactly the missed opportunity the hero avoids.

B. Heading structure

One H1: "Every number carries its source, its age and the person who defined it".

H2 in block order: "Two people, two numbers, half an hour" · "What Plumb puts next to a number" · "We store definitions, not your data" · "Questions". The card carries the metric name as an H3 inside the component, so the component's own heading level is fixed here rather than left to whichever page embeds it.

E. Optimisation checklist

  • One H1, the one above
  • LCP element: the live card. Server-rendered text rather than an image, which is the cheap half of the win. The honest risk is that it queries a real value at read time, so the card renders its definition, attribution and state immediately and fills the value in when it arrives, exactly as node 2.2 specifies. LCP must not wait on a warehouse
  • Crawlable <a> on every route out, including the example card and the trust page. No route that exists only as a script handler
  • Canonical present, no hreflang by the decision in A
  • No text baked into images. The card is a component, and the og:image is a rendering of it rather than the only place its content exists
  • No layout shift when the value arrives, which means its space is reserved before it is known
