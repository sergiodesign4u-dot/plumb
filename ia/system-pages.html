The 404 is not an edge case here, it is the reader's first contact

The reader's only entrance is a link, so a broken or expired one is where they meet us. This step also settles the one discrepancy the map has been carrying since the base layer, and the answer turns out to be a product decision rather than a legal one.

01

7.1, and it renders two ways from one node

The negative reference, quoted in full because it is the whole argument

dbt's documentation 404 says, in its entirety: "Page Not Found", "We could not find what you were looking for.", and "Please contact the owner of the site that linked you to the original URL and let them know their link is broken."

There is no route onward at all. The page tells the visitor to go and fix somebody else's website. Our reader is the person who was linked, so that sentence hands the problem to the one person who cannot solve it and has no account to solve it with.

The decision that stops the recovery block being a shrug, and it adds an object

"Ask whoever sent it" is only useful if we can say who that might be. For a link that never existed we know nothing. For a metric that existed and was removed, we could know, and until now we would have thrown it away.

So a deleted metric leaves a tombstone: its name, its owner, and the date it was removed. Nothing else. No definition, no value, no history. Three fields, storing nothing about the reader and nothing about the data, and it is the difference between "this is gone" and "this was Weekly active accounts, Dana Reyes removed it on 3 August, and she is who to ask". Recorded as a new decision at step 7 and added to the entity inventory in the map.

02

7.2 and 7.3, where the architecture becomes visible to the reader

The middle block of the 500 appears in none of the sources and follows from the product. An error page in a trust product has to say whether anything it showed can still be believed. Ours says the honest thing: nothing was shown, because we did not get the number. No stale value sits behind the error, because there is no stored copy for one to come from.

The separation that matters

A 500 is our failure. "Source is down" is a fact about the customer's warehouse and belongs on the card as node 2.4. These must never share wording, or a reader learns to read our outage as their data being broken.

7.3's sentence is specific to this product rather than generic. In a metadata-only product a maintenance window means the definitions are unreachable and the customer's warehouse is fine. It is the one moment the architecture is visible to the reader, whose mental model is that we hold the numbers.

Status codes are part of the specification: a real 404, a real 500, and 503 with Retry-After on 7.3. A soft 404 returning 200 is refused by name, because it is the version of this page that gets indexed and then competes with real pages.

7.4, and the discrepancy the map has been carrying is settled

The map records it openly: 7.4 is ПОТІМ because the market is US B2B and GDPR was deliberately deferred, but the public card with no account will almost certainly carry analytics, and at that point the question stops being legal and becomes a product question on the very screen where we promise to store nothing about the reader.

The decision, and it is a product decision rather than a legal one

The number card sets nothing on the reader's device. No cookie, no local storage, no third-party analytics, no tag manager.

A consent modal on the card would be a dialog about tracking sitting on top of a page whose whole claim is that we store nothing about the reader, and it would be the reader's first contact with us, arriving before the number they came for. So 7.4 never applies to the card. It stays ПОТІМ and applies only to cluster 6, where a cookie may legitimately exist and the visitor is not mid-task.

The correction this forces, and it is a correction rather than a refinement

Nodes 4.7 and 6.2 say we cannot count anything about readers. That was stated too broadly, and the precise version is better for both pages.

Nothing that identifies a reader is storedNo cookie, no device identifier, no per-reader log, no name, no address. Unchanged, and it is what the trust page sells
An anonymous counter on the metric is allowedServer side, holding no identity. It is what makes the activation metric measurable at all: activation is defined as the first time a card is opened by somebody other than the author, and a product that counted nothing could not observe its own activation
No count is ever shown in the productNode 4.7's banner still names no number, for a better reason than "we cannot": a count of links minted is not a count of people who will see the change, and putting it in front of the analyst invites them to read it as people

Both nodes are repaired upwards rather than left disagreeing with this one.

One mechanism lands here, and this is the only system page where that is true

P1, emotional. Stop feeling exposed when presenting a number I did not compute myself → trouble named in words rather than raised as an alarm → 7.1 block 2 and 7.2 block 2. A reader who followed a dead link did not break anything, and naming two possible causes without blaming them is the same discipline node 0.4 applies to a number's states.

Search is closed here rather than left implied. It is an affordance and not a node: it lives in node 0.1 as a persistent control over the registry, node 4.3 holds its result state including no-match, and node 3.1 records why the same control is not on the sources list. No screen was invented to fill a row, and this line exists so the absence reads as a decision.
